# Nemotron-H GEMM + lifecycle scan with the updated BoltBeam (2026-09-26)

The rescan the goal board asks for after the BoltBeam update: every Nemotron shape (decode M 8-128, prefill M 512-1024
pieces) against vLLM/cuBLAS and the roofline, the per-workload split (kernel sum vs wall, ours vs vLLM), the research
queue, the named lowering gaps ranked by recoverable time, the hi/lo effect on its own, and an ordered plan for goals
3 and 4. Measure-only: no product code changed. Nemotron 3 Nano 4B BF16, RTX 5090, tinygrad `exp` @ 6498ab04e,
BoltBeam `exp` @ beda801 (local only, not pushed), vLLM 0.30 / cuBLAS. All GPU numbers come from
`~/scratchpad/bin/gpu-run time` runs with the GPU otherwise idle; ncu ran as root inside `systemd-run -p MemoryMax=20G`.
Raw results and scripts: `bench/scan-20260926/` (raw ncu reports in `/home/ubuntu/storage/scan-20260926/`).

## 0. Headline

- **Decode (B=32/64/128): the gap is kernels, not lifecycle.** Wall gap +2.88 / +5.64 / +10.21 ms per step.
  GEMM kernels account for +0.93 / +3.42 / +7.18, non-GEMM kernels +1.68 / +1.88 / +2.47, lifecycle (wall minus
  kernel sum) +0.27 / +0.34 / +0.56. If every kernel matched vLLM, 90-95% of today's decode gap would be gone.
- **10k prefill: 1.65 s vs 0.38 s.** GEMMs +868 ms, Mamba kernels +339 ms, attention +47 ms, norm/residual +22 ms (and
  -5 ms where vLLM runs separate relu2/sampling kernels).
  Lifecycle is about 0 (the two sides measure wall differently; see section 2).
- **hi/lo is the biggest single decode lever, and parity can't be reached with it.** At B=128, vLLM's own cuBLAS
  kernels need +4.78 ms per step to run the doubled rows (10.50 vs 5.72 ms of GEMM). So no kernel work can bring
  hi/lo decode to vLLM parity at B>=128, and the same holds for prefill (+286 ms per 10k even with cuBLAS). At B=32,
  hi/lo is free (+0.12 ms), because cuBLAS pads M=32 to a 64-row tile anyway (same HMMA count, measured).
- **Searching the current derived space is nearly done for decode.** Climbs at 128/256 rows moved the promoted routes
  by 0-13%. The best route in the space is still 1.19-1.85x cuBLAS at the same row count. What's left needs new
  lowering or hi/lo removal; a better search inside today's space won't close it.
- **Prefill has a free, measured, search-only win.** Ring routes at 2048 rows, one launch per 1024-token piece
  instead of 4 sync2 chunks of 512, bring the best ssm_in to 1205 us vs 1898 us for today's GEMMs (1.14x cuBLAS at
  the same rows). Across all roles, prefill GEMMs drop from 1110 to 873 ms. With plain bf16 and 1024-row ring routes
  they drop to 405 ms (vLLM: 332).

Readings:
- **Decode (128/256 rows).** The best derived route is 0-13% better than the promoted one: ssm_out -13% / -9%, ssm_in
  -2% / -5%, ffn_down -1% / 0%, ffn_up and output 0%. That is worth about 0.4 ms per step at B=128. The in-space ceiling is
  1.20-1.85x cuBLAS at the same rows. On the memory-bound FFN and ssm_out shapes, cuBLAS reaches 69-79% of DRAM
  bandwidth and we reach 42-46%.
- **Prefill (1024/2048 rows).** A single ring launch per piece is 1.12-1.27x cuBLAS at the same rows (ssm_in 1.14x),
  against 1.8-2.2x for today's 512-row sync2 chunks. ncu replay of the ssm_in 2048 winner (`ncu/cand_ssm_in_2048.json`) vs
  cuBLAS `64x256_32x4` (`ncu/vncu_2048.json`): 1204 vs 1088 us under ncu (1.11x; the CUPTI timing above gives 1205 vs 1061, 1.14x), tensor pipe 86.5% vs 95.2%, identical HMMA count,
  BAR/HMMA 0.008 vs 0.019 (we already take fewer barriers), LDSM/HMMA 0.375 vs 0.251, LDS/HMMA 0.024 vs 0.052. The
  remaining 11% is fragment traffic per MMA. That is not a missing technique.
- **The prune.** BoltBeam's cost model keeps 11k-47k configs per shape (PRUNE_RATIO 6). Exhaustive runs would be hours
  per shape, and the climbs converged in 2-4 moves, so exhaustive isn't worth running on these shapes.

## 3. Research queue (unexplained residuals and unknown techniques)

The fresh ncu audit is `bench/scan-20260926/ncu/ncu_audit.md`. Ours are the promoted ring routes via the DEV=CUDA proxy
(`oncu.json`); the reference is fresh cuBLAS (`vncu.json`). Each gap is split into aux / rows (hi/lo) / wave tail /
stall excess, and the remainder is reported as unexplained. Ranked:

1. **Decode in-space ceiling on memory-bound shapes (not a bucket, measured).** At 128 rows, ffn_down / ffn_up /
   ssm_out reach 1.50-1.85x cuBLAS even after climbing. cuBLAS runs `128x64_64x3` / `256x64_32x4` with split-K 3 at
   69-79% of DRAM bandwidth. Ours runs 64x64 tiles (split-K 1-4) at 42-46% and stalls on math_pipe_throttle (~60%),
   although the same-row product is memory-bound. Next step: ncu of the climbed 128-row winners against cuBLAS at 128 rows (the
   current audit compares M tokens), per-CTA bytes in flight, and whether our split-K sum costs more than cuBLAS's
   `splitKreduce`.
2. **The fp32 matvec at M=16 (B=16 decode):** 35-45% of our time is unexplained on ffn_down (154 us), ssm_out (81),
   ffn_up (49), ssm_in (50), attn_o (41) and output (262). The stalls are lg_throttle 17-35% and grid.y=2 re-reads.
   Low priority, because B=16 is not a target workload. Routing M=9-16 through the 32-row ring routes would remove it
   (ssm_in 167 -> 83 us).
3. **ffn_up M=32: -44 us (the buckets over-attribute).** The wave_tail bucket charges 24.6 us for a 98-CTA grid, but
   the kernel is at math_pipe 54%. The wave-tail model is too coarse for grid < SM count. This is a bucket-model item
   for BoltBeam.
4. **BoltBeam importer defect, not a residual.** At M=512/1024 the unexplained values are -129 to -488 us, because the
   importer marks one chunk launch as the GEMM and the other 3 as aux. So the aux bucket over-counts (ssm_in M=1024
   "aux" 1681 us, when split + sum + concat measure 317 us). Fix in `ncu_counters.import_raw_csv`: mark every launch
   with a GEMM identity as GEMM. The prefill rows of this queue should be ignored until that is fixed. (Also seen: a
   target that resets `GlobalCounters` reports 0 launches in its SHAPE line. `cand_ncu.py` was imported by hand.)
5. **Prefill residual after the in-space best (ssm_in 2048, 1.11x under ncu, 1.14x CUPTI):** LDSM/HMMA 0.375 vs cuBLAS 0.251 at the same
   HMMA count, and tensor pipe 86.5% vs 95.2%. Candidates: ldmatrix width (`.x4` vs `.x2`) or warp-tile shape
   (cuBLAS 64x256 / 4 warps vs our 128x128 / 2x2). This is the "fragment reuse" item in section 4.

Unknown techniques are opcodes that cuBLAS executes and the BoltBeam taxonomy does not map. They are new in this
scan, which reads the executed-instance census; the 09-26 audit used a separate census file.
- `B2R` (3.2k-36k per kernel in `128x64_64x3`, `256x64_32x4`, `64x64_32x6`),
- `CGAERRBAR` (294-2000 per kernel, same kernels),
- `UVIMNMX` (640 / 8192 in the wmma `32x32_128x2` and `16x16_128x1` kernels).

These look like bookkeeping rather than throughput: a barrier-state read, a CGA error barrier at exit, and a uniform
min/max for tile bounds. They need a taxonomy entry. Only B2R has counts that scale with work, so check it first.

Technique gaps: the decode routes (M=32-128) now use cp.async (LDGSTS) and ldmatrix (LDSM), so the gap list from 09-26
is gone for decode. It remains for every prefill shape (the 512-row sync2 route) and for ssm_out M=64 (whose 128-row
route was kept at sync2).

## 5. The hi/lo effect, on its own (for Julian's pending decision)

The same shapes are measured four ways: our hi/lo route at 2M rows, our plain bf16 route at M rows (the fastest
promoted route at >= M rows, since 16/32-row bf16 routes are still sync2), and vLLM at M rows and at 2M rows. The last
column is what hi/lo would still cost if our kernels matched cuBLAS. Per-shape rows are in `hilo.md`, produced by
`scripts/hilo.py`.

| workload (GEMMs only) | ours hi/lo | ours plain bf16 | vLLM, M rows | vLLM, 2M rows | hi/lo tax with our kernels | hi/lo tax with cuBLAS kernels | kernel gap left at plain bf16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| decode B=32 (ms/step) | 6.23 | 6.11 | 4.85 | 4.93 | +0.12 | +0.08 | +1.26 |
| decode B=64 | 8.96 | 6.12 | 4.93 | 5.72 | +2.84 | +0.79 | +1.19 |

- At B=32, hi/lo costs 2% of the GEMM time. cuBLAS runs M=32 on a 64-row tile, so the HMMA count is the same (ncu:
  2.0x useful FLOPs on both sides).
- From B=64 up, hi/lo is exactly 2x HMMA. With our kernels it adds 2.8 ms (15% of the step) at B=64 and 5.6 ms (17%)

- What plain bf16 changes numerically (activation rounding to bf16 at every projection; the mismatch sources in
  `train-inference-mismatch-localization.md`) is not measured here. This is a speed-only input to the decision.

## 6. Proposed ordered plan for goals 3 and 4

Gains are per decode step at B=32 / 64 / 128 (baseline walls 11.69 / 18.72 / 32.12 ms) and per 10k prefill (baseline
1646 ms). "Measured" means a direct measurement in this scan; "est." means an ncu-bucket estimate.

**Decision first (Julian): hi/lo vs plain bf16, per phase.** Every later number depends on it. The options are: plain
bf16 for prompt prefill only; plain bf16 for decode B>=64 as well; or keep hi/lo and accept a parity target of vLLM at
2M rows.

Goal 4 (prefill), in order:
1. **Promote ring routes at the rows prefill issues** (2048 hi/lo rows or 1024 plain rows, one launch per piece). This
   uses the existing lowering and the existing gate. Measured in-space: GEMMs 1110 -> 873 ms with hi/lo (-237 ms,
   -14% wall), or 1110 -> 405 ms with plain bf16 (-705 ms, -43% wall). It also removes the chunk-concat aux kernels
   (ssm_in 153 us per piece).
2. **Plain-bf16 prompt prefill** (if the decision allows; goal 4 already asks for this test): included in the 405 ms
   above. It removes 140 ms of hi/lo split/sum aux kernels.
3. **Mamba prefill kernels: +339 ms** (376 vs 37 ms). This is the largest non-GEMM item, as big as the ssm_in GEMM gap.
   Three reduce kernels account for 297 ms (`r_2_2_96_...` 173 ms, `r_2_2_8_4_...` 64, `r_2_2_96_2_5_...` 59), against
   vLLM's `mamba_ssd_prefill` 26 ms. It needs its own audit (reverse-engineer vLLM's SSD chunk kernels first).
   Expected -250 to -330 ms.
4. **Attention (+47 ms) and norm/residual (+22 ms)**: fusion and the prefill attention kernel. Expected -50 to -65 ms.
5. **Lowering items** (fragment reuse, Stream-K, N pad; section 4): -25 to -55 ms (est.) after step 1.
Projection: 1646 -> about 940 (steps 1-2) -> about 610-690 (step 3) -> about 490-615 ms (steps 4-5; midpoint ~550), against vLLM's 377.
The remaining ~0.15-0.2 s is GEMMs at 1.12-1.27x cuBLAS plus lifecycle (753 graph launches, 15k kernel launches vs 1012). The
batched 10k memory failure is not covered by this scan.

Goal 3 (decode B=32-128), in order:
1. **(With plain bf16) a decode route table at M rows.** Promote ring routes at 16/32 bf16 rows: today the 32-row bf16
   route is the old sync2 one at 127 us, where the 64-row ring gives 82 us for ssm_in. Also apply the 128-row climb
   winners (ssm_out -13%). Measured: B=64 -2.8 ms (-15%), B=128 -5.6 ms (-17%), B=32 -0.1 ms.
2. **Non-GEMM decode kernels: +1.4 / +2.5 / +4.0 ms.** Sampling + bookkeeping (+0.36 / +0.83 / +1.90), attention
   (+0.37 / +0.75 / +1.03), norm/residual fusion (+0.54 / +0.58 / +0.59), state flush (+0.16 / +0.33 / +0.50). At B=128
   these are larger than every named lowering gap combined. They need the same kernel-level audit the GEMMs got
   (BoltBeam lifecycle already buckets them).
3. **Lowering: barrier per 64-K tile, then Stream-K, then ragged K** (section 4): -1.1 to -1.5 ms per step (est.).
   This is the only way past the 1.2-1.85x in-space ceiling, and it needs a prototype for each item before any claim.
4. **Lifecycle: one graph per step (5.09 -> 1) and fewer launches (817-867 -> about 350)**: -0.27 / -0.34 / -0.56 ms
   (measured term; the fusion part sits in step 2).
5. **Climb winners at hi/lo rows** (if hi/lo stays): ssm_in / ssm_out at 256 rows, about -0.4 ms at B=128 (measured,
   microbench).
Projection at B=128 with plain bf16: 32.1 -> ~26.5 (step 1) -> ~22.5 (step 2) -> ~21 (steps 3-4) ms, against vLLM's
21.9. At B=32, steps 2-4 carry the gain: 11.7 -> ~8.5-9.3 ms (the estimates overlap), against vLLM's 8.8.
